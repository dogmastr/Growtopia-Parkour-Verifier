Bounce cancel

A trampoline throws you up at 800 px/s. A bounce cancel is replacing that bounce with your own jump: with Double Jump, press jump during the bounce and your upward speed is set to 450 px/s, whatever is left of the 800.

In the verifier: yes. The Mushroom and the Diving Board are in the editor (keys M and B), and the route checker tries holding and letting go on every frame, so it finds cancel timing by itself.

The cancel

  • A fresh jump press during the bounce spends your Double Jump air jump. It sets your upward speed to 450 px/s (it doesn't add), and the 800 is gone.
  • So the press makes you slower while you are still rising faster than 450 px/s, and gives you a fresh jump from wherever you are once you are below that. Pressed at the very top, it is a free extra jump of about 101 px.
  • That is how you pick your own height off a trampoline: bounce, then press when you are at the speed you want to start from.
  • A press on the landing frame itself can't be a ground jump, because the bounce has already taken you off the ground. With wings it spends the air jump the same way.

Shortening the bounce by letting go

Without wings, or with the air jump already spent, you can still cut the rise short with the jump button:

  • Hold the whole way: you rise about 320 px (10 tiles) and reach the top after 0.8 s.
  • Let go straight away: you rise only about 53 px (a bit under 2 tiles) and reach the top after 0.13 s.
  • Holding through the landing is what gives the big bounce. If you are not holding when you land, you get the short one on its own.
  • You can change your mind at any moment. Let go, or press again, and the slowing-down rate switches from that frame on.

Why it works

  • Landing on a trampoline gives your Double Jump air jump back, and the bounce itself doesn't touch it.
  • The trampoline sets your upward speed to 800 px/s and leaves everything else alone, including whether jump is held.
  • So the ordinary jump rule applies on the way up. While you rise with jump held you slow down at 1000 px/s². While you rise with jump let go, 5000 more is added, so you slow down at 6000 px/s².

Inside the frame after you stop on top of the block, things happen in this order:

  1. The ground check marks you on the ground, and Double Jump's air jump comes back.
  2. Gravity zeroes your downward speed, because you are on the ground.
  3. The contact test runs the trampoline: upward speed is set to 800 and you are off the ground.
  4. Jump handling happens. A press here is not a ground jump any more.
  5. The move happens: you rise 13 px in this same frame.

The whole order of a frame is under The player.

63fps, Frame time and frame rate

The game has no fixed step. One frame on screen is one step of movement, and that step is a whole number of milliseconds: 16 ms every frame when the counter shows 63 fps, 17 ms every frame when it shows 59, and a mix of the two in between.

In the verifier: yes for a steady 16 ms and a steady 17 ms. The Frame length buttons in its Constants panel pick one (it starts at 16 ms), and the game there, the route checker and its replays all step at it. The mix of 16 and 17, any other frame length and the 100 ms cap: not yet. The numbers on the rest of this page are for 16 ms.

What each FPS reading means

Where this comes from: simulated from the timer rules below.

  • The FPS counter counts updates until more than 1000 ms have passed. So it reads a little high, and 1000 ÷ (FPS shown) is not the frame time.
  • The shortest input is one frame. That frame is worth 16 ms or 17 ms of movement, depending on the row above.
  • With a steady 16 ms or a steady 17 ms, a held jump rises 104 px. It lasts 59 frames at 16 ms and 56 frames at 17 ms.
  • Higher frame rates give shorter steps: at 120 Hz with no limit, 8 and 9 ms mixed. This only happens if the 60 fps limit is not working.

How the game times a frame

Where this comes from: read from the Android game code.

  • There is no fixed physics step. One update is one frame.
  • The physics uses the frame time ÷ 1000 as the time for that frame. It is always a whole number of milliseconds. 16.67 never happens.
  • A single frame is capped at 100 ms.
  • The game asks for a 60 fps limit. The waiting itself is done outside this code.

Each frame, the timer does this:

  1. It reads the system clock, in whole milliseconds.
  2. It takes the time since the last frame. If that is more than 100 ms, it is cut to 100.
  3. It averages the last 8 of those times and rounds the average down to a whole millisecond. That is the frame time.
  4. It compares this frame's own time with the frame time. If it was 1, 2, 3 or 4 ms longer, that leftover goes into the next frame. Any other difference is dropped.

How high a jump goes at each frame rate

Where this comes from: worked out frame by frame from the rules on this page. At 16 ms it gives the page's own numbers: 104, 325, 123 and 67 px. High Jump uses the measured gravity of about 713.

In the verifier: not yet. It only steps at 16 or 17 ms.

The lower the frame rate, the higher a jump goes. Some jumps gain a whole tile:

  • High Jump reaches 5 tiles from 12 fps (81 ms frames).
  • A jump started in water reaches 4 tiles from 19 fps (52 ms).
  • The Antigravity Generator reaches 9 tiles from 12 fps (82 ms).
  • A trampoline bounce reaches 11 tiles from 12 fps (81 ms).
  • A lava bounce reaches 3 tiles from 12 fps (83 ms).
  • A plain held jump never reaches 4 tiles. It tops out at 124 px, 4 px short.

How it is worked out:

  • Gravity is applied before the move.
  • A jump or a bounce sets its speed before that frame's move.
  • Each move is whole pixels (trunc(v + 0.5)), and the leftover is carried to the next frame.
  • The frame time is whole milliseconds, capped at 100.

A longer frame moves you a whole frame at full speed before gravity catches up, so the top comes out higher.

  • Below 10 fps the lines are flat. The game cuts each frame to 100 ms, so it just plays in slow motion: same height, more real time per frame.
  • The lines go up in steps because frames are whole milliseconds and moves are whole pixels.

Why 63 fps appears after the game has been open a long time

Where this comes from: a likely explanation, not confirmed. It is read from the public Proton SDK engine source, which Growtopia is built on (shared/win/app/main.cpp and shared/win/WinUtils.cpp).

  • The public Windows loop keeps the time of the next frame in a 32-bit float: fpsTimer = float(now) + 1000.0f / 60. It waits while fpsTimer > now. now is whole milliseconds since the game started.
  • A 32-bit float holds whole numbers exactly only up to 224 = 16,777,216. Past that it can only hold every 2nd number, then every 4th, and so on, doubling each time the number doubles.
  • So the 16.67 ms wait gets rounded to whatever step the float can still hold.

Blocks

Trampolines

Blocks that throw you up at 800 px/s when you land on them. The Mushroom is the plain one.

In the verifier: the Mushroom and the Diving Board. The others follow the same rule and could be added as tiles. The Steam Launcher and trampolines on fire: not yet.

The rule

If gravity is on, you are not moving up, and your feet are at or above the top of the block: your upward speed becomes 800 px/s and you are no longer on the ground.

The speed is set, not added, so how fast you were falling doesn't matter.

What the bounce leaves alone

Your sideways speed, whether jump is held, the ledge grace frame, slippery, your stored air jump, and knockback. Only your upward speed and "on the ground" change. That is why bounce cancel works.

When it is checked

  • In the contact test, which runs after gravity and before jump handling and the move.
  • Only the up-and-down test counts. It looks at your box moved down 1 px when you are standing, or down by this frame's fall (at most 1 px). If that box overlaps the 32 × 32 tile at all, even by 1 px of width, the rule runs.
  • From the side a solid trampoline is an ordinary wall. There is no bounce.
  • Once one trampoline has fired, the game stops looking at other blocks in that test. That includes a spike to its right: see Which block wins.

The frames around a landing

  1. Frame N: your move ends with you stopped right on top of the block.
  2. Frame N+1: the ground check marks you on the ground (and gives the air jump back), gravity zeroes your downward speed, the contact test bounces you (−800, off the ground), then jump handling and the move happen.
  3. So a jump press on frame N+1 can't be a ground jump. It only sets "jump held".
  4. The air jump from that frame is yours any time after the bounce. It sets your upward speed to 450, replacing what is left of the 800.

Blocks

Lemon Jelly Block

The Lemon Jelly Block (5392) bounces you by how fast you fall onto it: the faster the fall, the stronger the bounce, from 531 to 1200 px/s.

In the verifier: not yet.

When it bounces

The same landing condition as a trampoline: gravity on, not rising, feet at or above its top.

How strong

  • r = (fall speed ÷ 400)²
  • Your upward speed becomes 1200 × r, but never less than 531.25. So 531 to 1200 px/s.
  • The floor of 531.25 applies for fall speeds up to about 266 px/s (about 35 px of fall).

The rise afterwards

  • While you rise, gravity is multiplied by (1 − boost).
  • The boost starts at r and is multiplied by 0.5625 every frame.
  • It ends on the first frame you are no longer rising.

Not known

Which stored fall speed the block reads. It is assumed to be your speed just before landing.

Pinball bumper

A solid block that bats you away from its centre at a fixed 500 px/s. Unlike a trampoline, it also makes the game forget you are holding jump.

In the verifier: yes for the solid bumper. The Bouncy Turret and the fire flag: not yet.

What it does

Your velocity is replaced by 500 px/s pointing from the block's centre to the centre of your box. Your old speed is thrown away. You become slippery, and you are taken off the ground, the ledge grace frame is cleared, and jump held is cleared.

After the launch

  • Upward launches are short. Jump held is cleared, so the jump-cut gravity (6000 px/s²) applies even if your finger is still on the key. A straight-up launch rises only about 21 px.
  • Pressing jump again changes that. A fresh press sets jump held again, so gravity drops to 1000 and the same launch can reach about 125 px. With Double Jump the press spends your air jump instead and sets you to −450.
  • Sideways speed is kept. While you are slippery there is no friction, so 500 px/s lasts until you land. Steering is 15% strength while you rise and full strength while you fall, and only below your normal top speed.
  • Walls bounce you. Hitting a wall while slippery reverses you at 70% of your speed.
  • Landing ends it. The ground check works slippery out from the block you land on, so friction comes back unless it is ice.
  • Ground-level side hits: you are back on the ground one frame later, so friction returns straight away. With no key held you slide about 104 px.

When it fires

  • Each frame, before you move, the game tests your box moved sideways by this frame's run, then moved up or down by this frame's rise or fall. A bumper overlapping either test fires.
  • The downward test reaches only 1 px, so a top hit fires the frame after you land on it.
  • The sideways and upward tests reach a whole frame of movement, so they fire about 4 px early at walking speed and about 7 px early on a jump from below.
  • Standing still against its side does nothing. You have to be moving into it.
  • There is no cooldown on the physics (only the sound has one, 150 ms).
  • It ends the contact check, so a spike to its right never gets a turn. See Which block wins.

In water

The 500 px/s sideways speed survives, because slippery blocks friction. A downward launch is cut to 120 px/s at once, and an upward one rises about 69 px.

Which blocks

Solid: Pinball Bumper (526), Lime Jelly Block (5386), Orange Jelly Block (5390), HyperTech Multi-Jet Energy Pulser (7160) and White Grape Jelly Block (7440). Platform: the Bouncy Turret (13624), which you can pass through from below and from the sides.

The Bouncy Turret

  • It is a platform, so your box can be inside it.
  • Both contact checks run every frame, even when you are not moving, and the bumper rule has no direction condition.
  • So it fires every frame your box overlaps it.

The fire flag

If the bumper's tile has tile flag bit 12 set (TILE_FLAG_ON_FIRE), it hurts like lava instead. For a player who is immune to pain blocks, a burning bumper is a bumper again.

Blocks

Spikes

Death Spikes kill the moment the game's contact test sees them. The whole 32 × 32 square counts, whatever the artwork shows.

In the verifier: yes, as one Spike tile that is always deadly. Blocks that are deadly only while switched on, and Spikey Leaves: not yet.

What that means in practice

  • Standing on a block next to spikes: you die if even 1 px of your box overlaps the spike's column. The 1 px downward test overlaps the spike's top.
  • Standing right up against the side of spikes is safe. You die on the first frame whose sideways movement rounds to 1 px or more into them.
  • Landing on spikes, jumping up into them and running into their side all kill the same way.
  • A trampoline or a bumper to the left of a spike gets its turn first and saves you, even with most of your box over the spike. One to the right doesn't. See Which block wins.

What counts as touching

  • Each frame the game works out where your box would be after this frame's movement. If that box overlaps the spike's square by at least 1 px, you die, that frame.
  • Edges that only touch don't count.

Dying

The game only sends a respawn request that names the block. The server decides where and when you come back.

At a checkpoint

In this verifier you appear with your head at the top of the checkpoint's tile, so your feet are 2 px above whatever is below. A spike right under a checkpoint therefore doesn't kill you on the first frame.

Blocks

Lava and other pain blocks

Lava doesn't simply kill you the way spikes do. It throws you away from it and puts you in knockback, and enough hits in a row kill you.

In the verifier: not yet.

From above or below

  • If gravity is off, your upward speed becomes 50 px/s.
  • Falling or standing: your upward speed becomes 800 px/s.
  • Rising (you jumped up into it): your vertical speed is reversed, so you are sent back down at the same speed.
  • You are off the ground.
  • If you weren't moving sideways, you are given a tiny push the way you face. Then your sideways speed is made at least 300 px/s, keeping its direction.
  • You are in knockback.

From the side

Your sideways speed is reversed, and unless you are already in knockback it is made at least 300 px/s. Nothing else changes: not your vertical speed, not whether you are on the ground, not knockback. A side hit on another pain block while in knockback only flips your speed, with no 300 minimum.

Knockback

The bounce is only about 2 blocks high and 3 blocks long.

Only an up-or-down hit starts it. It ends when the ground check finds ground. While it lasts:

  • Gravity is much stronger. Rising with jump held: 4250 extra. Rising with jump let go: 5000 extra. Falling: 4250 extra. The fall cap stays 400.
  • Steering is only 15% as strong, and nothing slows you down (no friction).
  • A normal wall barely bounces you: your speed × −0.01.

Dying

The client doesn't simulate damage. It reports each hit, and the server decides. A player dies after making contact with lava 5 times in a row within 5 seconds, or 10 times with the Fireproof mod. Bouncing on a lava floor takes 21 to 23 frames a time (about a third of a second), so the 5th bounce kills.

When blocks sit side by side

When your box is over two different blocks at once, the game doesn't weigh them. It looks at them one at a time, from the left, and the first one that does something ends the check.

In the verifier: yes, for spikes, bumpers and trampolines. Lava: not yet.

How the check goes

  1. The game lists every block your box overlaps in the contact test (at least 1 px of overlap each).
  2. It goes through the list column by column, from left to right. Inside one column the upper block goes first.
  3. Each block gets its turn. A spike kills you, so nothing after it matters. A trampoline bounces you, a bumper throws you and a pain block (lava) knocks you back. Each of those counts as handled and ends the check: the blocks after it never get a turn.
  4. The bounce or the throw puts you in the air, so on the next frame you aren't touching either block.

In the downward check, plain blocks like dirt do nothing, so they don't take a turn.

Two lists, every frame

  • The sideways check and the up-and-down check are two separate lists. The sideways one goes first.
  • Both run every frame, even if you did not move.
  • A plain wall takes its turn without ending the check. It sets your sideways speed to 0 (on slippery ground it reverses it at 70%, in knockback at 1%), and the check goes on to the next block.

Other pairs

  • Lava and a trampoline: lava also ends the check, so whichever of the two is further left wins.
  • A bumper and a spike: a bumper ends the check too. A bumper on the left throws you before the spike is looked at. A spike on the left kills you.
  • Sideways: the sideways check is its own list and works the same way. It is separate from the downward one, so each stops at its own first handled block.
  • A wall above lava, in the same column: in the sideways check the wall is the upper block, so it goes first and sets your sideways speed to 0. Then the lava sees a speed of 0 and sets it to 300 px/s to the right. So the push is to the right, whichever side you came from.

Trying it in the game

  1. Put a Mushroom and a spike side by side on flat ground, with the Mushroom on the left.
  2. Drop onto them so that most of your box is over the Mushroom and a few pixels are over the spike. The code says you bounce.
  3. Swap them, and drop on the same way. The code says you die.

The verifier's Mushroom, spike and pinball tiles follow this rule, so you can set the same thing up in Build.

Left to right, and top to bottom inside a column, is read from the code.

Water

Water isn't a block. It is a flag on a tile, so a tile can be a block and water at once, and you pass through it.

In the verifier: yes for the water itself, with the Jet Ski and floaties as gear. The float items that last 3 s and the Fishing Dinghy: not yet. The route checker can also change the water item on the way when you ask it to: see Changing mods. Mud and glue aren't there, so nothing stacks with the water speed yet.

When you count as in water

Only the middle point of your box matters. You are in water while that point is in a water tile, which is when you are about half under. The game goes back to normal the first frame it is out.

Up and down

  • Gravity and the fall cap are 0.3 times as much: 300 px/s² when sinking, and a fall cap of 120 px/s that you reach after 0.4 s and 24 px.
  • Entering fast, your fall speed is cut to 120 on the first frame. Upward speed isn't capped.
  • A jump is 0.6 times as strong (−270 px/s). You can jump from the bottom, or in open water any time you are sinking. So swimming up is: jump, wait until you start sinking, jump again.
  • Held, one jump rises about 121 px over 0.9 s, a bit higher than a jump on land. Let go and it is about 20 px.
  • The parasol and gliding are off in water.

Jumping into water from dry ground

  • A jump from a dry tile, with water starting one tile up, is a full 450 jump under 0.3 gravity: it rises 308 px. Started in water: 123 px. On land: 104 px.
  • A trampoline bounce with the middle of your body in water rises 1072 px, against 325 px dry.
  • The jump's strength is decided on the frame of the press, from whether the middle of your body is in water then.
  • Water gravity applies on every frame the middle of your body is in water.

In 16 ms frames a jump comes out a little higher than the smooth maths says: 123 px against 121 in water, 104 against 101 on land.

Sideways

  • Top speed becomes the water speed (usually 125 px/s). Acceleration and friction are unchanged.
  • Top speed stacks: water sets the base to the water speed, then mud multiplies it by 0.6 and glue by 0.5. See Ice and slow ground.
  • Entering at 250, friction brings you down to 125 in about 0.1 s.
  • A held run averages about 115 px/s, not 125: at top speed, friction and acceleration take turns frame by frame. The same quirk gives about 240 on land.

Float items

Surfboards, the Fishing Dinghy, Jet Skis, the Swan Boat and others: the table further down lists them all. They are checked on the frame you enter the water.

  • While you float, gravity and the fall cap are both zero, so you stop at the surface, half under.
  • With gravity at 0, an upward speed is kept for the whole water column.
  • You can jump at any time, not just while sinking, and a jump keeps its full −270 until your middle point leaves the water.
  • Floating ends 3 s after you enter (never for the Fishing Dinghy, the Jet Ski and the Golden Jet Ski), or when you touch the ground. Changing what you wear can end it too: see below.
  • The timer restarts each time you come back in, so hopping out and in should keep you afloat.

The verifier has the Jet Ski only (it stands for the Golden Jet Ski too): a float with no time limit, which any Gear button ends, and which a water item change by the route checker ends too. The float items that last 3 s and the Fishing Dinghy aren't in it.

When a float starts and ends

Floating is a state of its own, kept apart from being in water, and only one thing starts it:

  • It starts on the frame you enter the water: the frame the middle of your body is in a water tile and wasn't on the frame before. On that frame the game looks at what you wear on your feet and in your hand, and starts the float if one of them is a float item.
  • Everything else only ends it: the ground, the time running out, and a clothing update that your new outfit doesn't pass.
  • So a float item put on while you are already in the water does nothing until the middle of your body leaves the water and comes back in. You have to be out for a frame.
  • A mod change can't stand in for entering the water: neither of the updates it brings touches whether you count as in water. See Changing mods.

Each time your clothes change, the game tests the outfit it has just been sent, and ends the float unless the item in your hand or the one on your feet is one of 12 that pass. That test is narrower than the one on entering the water: the Jet Ski and the Golden Jet Ski start a float, but they are not among the 12.

Platforms and collision kinds

Every block has a collision kind, and the kind decides which sides stop you. A platform is any block of kind 2: you stand on it, and you can pass through it from every other side.

In the verifier: yes for platforms: the Platform tile and the Diving Board follow the rules below. One-Way Blocks, doors and gates: not yet.

What that means

T is the top edge of the platform.

  • Falling through: if your feet are already below T, nothing stops you and you fall through. That is the case when you walked in from the side, or when your jump topped out inside it.
  • Jumping up through one: you land only if your feet get to T or higher. One pixel short and you drop back through. With a 104 px jump that is a platform up to 3 tiles above the floor, 6 tiles with Double Jump.
  • Block effects still use the whole 32 × 32 square: platform trampolines bounce, the Bouncy Turret bumps, Spikey Leaves kill. A platform trampoline such as the Diving Board still needs your feet at or above its top, and you not rising.
  • No dropping down: there is no key for dropping down through a platform.
  • Only the top row matters for movement. The lower 31 px do nothing.

The rules

There is no single platform routine: each of the places below treats kind 2 specially.

  • Sideways: a platform never stops you.
  • Upward: a platform never stops you. There is no ceiling under one, so there is no hovering under one.
  • Downward: the game moves you 1 px at a time. A platform stops a downward pixel only when your feet are exactly at T before that pixel. So a fall from above always lands, at any speed.
  • Standing: you count as on the ground only when your feet are exactly at T. The game then snaps your height to a whole pixel. Otherwise the ground check ends as "not on the ground".
  • Never a wall: a platform does not zero your sideways speed, it cannot crush you, and it cannot be the wall for wall climbing.

Which blocks are platforms

158 blocks are platforms. Besides the plain ones (Wooden Platform, Ladder, Staircase, Climbing Vine, tables, roofs) that includes 8 trampolines, 4 ice-type blocks, the Bouncy Turret and Spikey Leaves.

In the verifier: yes for running, jumping, falling and moving: this is what its physics is built from. One difference: it works out gravity and sideways speed before the ground check, not after it, so a fall off a ledge starts one frame later than in the order below. The Gravity Well, the block triggers, the 16-second key release and the special states: not yet.

The box

20 px wide and 30 px high. That size comes from testing, not from the code. In the game the player's position is the box's top-left corner; the verifier follows the middle of the feet.

Running

  • Left and right add or subtract acceleration. Both keys together cancel out.
  • You speed up toward top speed. With no key, or already at top speed, friction pulls you toward 0 at 1200 px/s². So a held run alternates between top speed and one braking step, and averages about 240.
  • Standstill to full speed takes about 0.21 s, and full speed to a stop the same.
  • A direction key held for 16 seconds is released by the game and has to be pressed again. With Slithering Slippers (9420) or Present Goblin Disguise (4260) that does not happen, and with no key held you keep running the way you face.

Jumping

  • A jump sets your upward speed to 450 px/s. It doesn't add.
  • A full held jump rises about 101 px (3.2 tiles) and takes 0.45 s to reach the top. Let go and gravity is 6000 px/s²: a one-frame tap hops about 20 px.
  • In 16 ms frames the held jump comes out at 104 px. The table is under How far and how high.
  • A press and a release that both happen between two frames give no jump.

Falling

Gravity is 1000 px/s² in the air and nothing on the ground. Falls stop speeding up at 400 px/s (12.5 tiles per second), which you reach after 0.4 s and 80 px.

Moving, walls and floors

  • Each frame the box moves a whole number of pixels, one pixel at a time, sideways first and then up or down, and stops at the first block. The leftover fraction of a pixel carries to the next frame.
  • A wall zeroes your sideways speed (on slippery ground it bounces you back at 70%). Landing zeroes your downward speed.
  • Nothing zeroes your upward speed at a ceiling. The move is simply blocked each frame, so you stay pinned and your speed fades under gravity. See Ceilings.
  • The game rounds each frame's movement in a way that favours right and down. Leftward and upward movement lags by up to 1 px, so a pixel-exact jump can work in one direction only.

When a press jumps

The full list, in the order the game checks:

  1. On the ground: jump. Nothing is spent.
  2. On the ledge grace frame (the frame after you walk off a ledge): jump. A stored air jump is cleared.
  3. With a stored air jump: jump. The air jump is cleared.
  4. With the middle of your body in water while sinking (or while floating on a float item): jump.
  5. While falling, with the middle of your body in an Antigravity Field, or with the Antigravity Generator switched on anywhere in the world: jump.
  • Ledge grace uses up the air jump. The game handles that press like an air jump. So with Double Jump the grace frame gives nothing extra: one press, one jump, air jump gone. Without Double Jump it gives one jump, one frame late.
  • The grace jump's strength is the usual one (−450, or −270 under the × 0.6 conditions).
  • The frame before it, the last one still on the ground, is an ordinary ground jump and keeps the air jump. See Ledges and gaps.
  • Mars weather is not in this list. It gives lower gravity only. See Low gravity.

The verifier follows the ledge grace rule.

One frame, in order

  1. Water check: is the middle of your body in a water tile.
  2. Ground check.
  3. Controller step: read the keys, then gravity, then sideways speed.
  4. Pull from a switched-on Gravity Well, if one is in range.
  5. Contact test: the sideways list first, then the up-and-down list.
  6. Block triggers at the middle of your body: crush check, checkpoints, portals, Steam Stomper.
  7. Jump handling.
  8. Move: sideways pixels first, then up or down.

Changing mods

Putting an item on or taking one off changes your mods, and every such change takes away your stored double jump. In the air it stays gone until you land.

In the verifier: yes when you play: a Gear button takes the stored air jump away at once, and ends a Jet Ski's float. In the route checker only for the water items, and only with "Water items can be changed on the way" ticked. It may then put one on, take it off or swap it on any frame, at least 21 frames after the last change (20 in 17 ms frames), and each change costs the air jump. The other gear stays as ticked for a whole route. The checker picks the frame a change lands on, and doesn't work out the wait for the server.

What you see

  • On the ground a mod change costs nothing. The ground check stores the air jump again on the next frame.
  • In the air the double jump is gone until you land. That is so even when the item has nothing to do with Double Jump, as long as it changes a mod.
  • Putting a Double Jump item on in the air gives no air jump either. The update that turns Double Jump on is the same one that clears the stored jump.
  • With the stored jump gone, a jump press in the air does nothing. The mid-air jumps that don't need it still work: see "When a press jumps" under The player.

Why

The game keeps the double jump as one stored air jump. Only five things ever change it:

WhenThe stored air jump
A player is createdEmpty
The ground check finds you on the groundStored, but only with Double Jump
A press jumps in one of the special mid-air cases: in an Antigravity Field, or with the Antigravity Generator on (see Low gravity)Stored just before the jump
An air jump or a ledge grace jump firesEmptied
The server's update of your character's state arrivesEmptied, always

The last row is the cause. When the server answers a mod change it sends your character's state again. The game takes in the new mods and then clears the stored air jump, without looking at what changed. It doesn't check whether you still have Double Jump.

The message that changes the clothes you are seen in is a separate one, and it leaves the air jump alone.

Sending a change

  • Each change takes two taps: one to select the item, and one on the selected item to wear it or take it off. After a change is sent the selection goes back to the Fist, so the next change needs both taps again.
  • The game sends a change at most once every 333 ms. A tap inside that time is dropped, not kept for later.
  • The wait is shared by every item. Going back and forth between two items doesn't get around it.
  • Nothing changes on the frame of the tap. The game sends the request at once and waits. Your mods and your stored air jump only change when the server's answer arrives, so a change lands one trip to the server and back after the tap.

The game counts that time in whole frames (see 63fps, Frame time and frame rate), so the 333 ms comes out as a number of frames:

Frame lengthFrames until the next change can be sent
Every frame 16 ms21 (336 ms)
Every frame 17 ms20 (340 ms)
A mix of 16 and 17 ms20, or 21 when the last two frames are both 16 ms

Odd cases

  • Answers aren't held back. Each frame the game takes in every state update that has arrived, however many there are. Two can land in the same frame, and one a frame for many frames in a row would all be taken.
  • With a parasol or a glide item, holding jump after the change glides at once. There is no air jump left for the press to spend first. See Gear.
  • A float item put on in the water does nothing until the middle of your body leaves the water and comes back in. Floating only starts on the frame you enter the water, and a mod change doesn't count as entering. See Water.
  • A change can end a float that is running. With a Jet Ski or a Golden Jet Ski any change of clothes ends it, even one that leaves the Jet Ski on. With the other float items it keeps running as long as one of them is still worn after the change. See "When a float starts and ends" under Water.

Not known

  • How long the trip to the server and back takes. It depends on the connection.
  • What else makes the server send a state update. Anything that does clears the stored air jump the same way.
  • Whether a mod change takes away the second jump you have right after a checkpoint. The verifier leaves it.

Ledges and gaps

What happens in the frames around the end of a block: the last jump you can make from it, the one frame of ledge grace after it, and running across a gap without jumping.

In the verifier: yes for the edge jump, ledge grace and the two layouts below: the checker gives the same results. Gap running comes out differently there. See the note at the end of this topic.

Edge jump and ledge grace

An edge jump is a jump pressed on the last frame you are still on the ground: the last frame with at least 1 px of your box over the block you are leaving. It is an ordinary ground jump, as late as it can be. Because it is a ground jump, it does not use the Double Jump air jump. Ledge grace, also called coyote time, is the frame after it.

The three frames around a ledge, with Double Jump:

  • The edge jump and ledge grace are two different frames, one after the other. Ledge grace is exactly one frame long.
  • The edge jump is the latest press that still leaves you a second jump.
  • On the grace frame the game handles the press like an air jump. If a Double Jump air jump is stored, it is cleared. So with Double Jump the grace frame gives nothing extra: one press, one jump, air jump gone.
  • Without Double Jump, both frames give one jump.
  • The jump's strength is the usual one (−450, or −270 under the × 0.6 conditions).

For the same jump out of a column with a One-Way Block in it, see One-Way Block edge jump.

Why the frame order matters

Inside one frame the game does, in this order: ground check, gravity and sideways speed, contact test, jump, move. The move goes sideways first, then up or down.

  • The contact test runs before the jump. While you stand, its up-and-down check only looks 1 px below you.
  • On an edge-jump frame, at most about 4 px of your box is still over the block, and the sideways step is about 4 px. So the sideways step carries your box clear of the block's column, and only then does the upward step happen (7 px on the jump frame).

A block overhead

A floor block, one tile of air, then a plain block directly above. Standing in the gap, your head is 2 px under the upper block.

  • Edge jump: the sideways step clears the column first, so nothing is above you when you rise. Full jump, air jump kept.
  • Pressing early is safe here. Your head hits the block after 2 px and you are pinned, but nothing cancels your upward speed. It fades by 16 px/s per frame while jump is held (96 per frame if you let go). You keep sliding sideways, and when your box clears the column you go on up with what is left.

Why the edge jump is safe:

  • The contact test on that frame runs before the jump and only looks 1 px down. The spike is 2 px above your head, so it is not seen.
  • The sideways step then clears the spike's column, and you rise beside the spike. Edges that only touch don't count.
  • On the next frame both checks use your new position, which is clear of the column.

Why one frame early kills:

  • After the sideways step, 1 to 4 px of your box is still under the spike. The spike stops the upward step after 2 px. Being stopped does not kill; only the contact test does.
  • On the next frame, the up-and-down check looks where your box would be after that frame's rise (about 7 px up), at your current sideways position. That box overlaps the spike. You die.

So the window to go up and keep the Double Jump is one 16 ms frame. To get up alive at all, there are two frames: the edge jump and ledge grace.

Ceilings

Hitting a ceiling doesn't take your upward speed away. You stay pinned under it until gravity has used the speed up, and while you are pinned you don't fall.

In the verifier: yes. A block above you, a waterfall and the top edge of the world all pin you this way.
